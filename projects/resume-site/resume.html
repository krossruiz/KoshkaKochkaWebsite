I'm a self-taught programmer, and I've been programming since high school, so it's been about 10 years. I first taught myself Android app programming with Java from a book at the local library, and I learned computer science basics through a UNSW Sydney Course by Richard Buckland as well as learning Java through YouTube channel called thenewboston.

I attended College of the Canyons in Santa Clarita and was a part of their computer club. We tried working on building video games together with the Unity game engine. I did 3D modelling and integrated assets from artists into the game. We never had a finished product. I did complete one small game though, a simple fighting game called Punch Bois that I did the programming for and my friend Bradley Wood did the art assets and physics tuning for.

Download for Windows (.exe)

I've worked as a math tutor, which is my main source of income, for about as long as I've been a programmer. I started at Whiz Tutor, an app for tutors, like Uber, and I even programmed for them. Unfortunately I couldn't seriously commit to the job and that didn't last long, but I did learn about progressive web apps, the 3 web languages, TypeScript, Angular, React, RESTful APIs, sockets, and it laid out many foundations for what I know today.

I've been working as a math tutor at Verdugo Hills High School for the past year for a company called EduCare. I also worked with them 2019-2020 before and during COVID.

I hadn't programmed in a few years, but I picked it up again about a year ago when I bought myself a new laptop and DeepSeek R1 had just come out. I started experimenting with AI tools and since then have used Cursor, Google Antigravity, and Claude Code, along with models from different providers as they've come out.

Some things I've worked on include a Chrome extension that filters YouTube recommended videos, small tools to try and speed up renaming scanned documents at work, and experiments with WebXR for my Quest 3.

I go out to Meetups and talk with other programmers and learn about their projects and workflows, and I share what I've learned too.

Aside from a few pushes to the master branch of the Whiz Tutor app I've never released code publicly, but I did recently have this site hosted on my Github. It was made for Pi Day.
